What they can do

Calum Organ v1.4

A step sequencer for Calum's pipe organ: each pipe has a valve driven by an ESP32 board, and the boards play together over ESP-NOW. Draw a pattern and the organ plays it. Without the organ, a simulated one plays it here in the browser, placed around you in a room you arrange.

People

  • Organ and concept: Calum Builder
  • Software: Miguel Angel Crozzoli
  • Built with Claude AI as a co-designing agent.

Sound

  • SuperCollider (scsynth, GPL-3.0-or-later), compiled to WebAssembly by SuperSonic (Sam Aaron; client MIT), running in your browser.
  • Recorded organ: a chapel organ sampled by Simon Dalzell (Ivy Audio), from Versilian Studios Chamber Orchestra 2: Community Edition, CC0 (public domain). 17 notes, trimmed, looped and levelled for this app.
  • Pipe synthesis, the stopped-pipe model measured from those recordings, and the headphone placement: SuperCollider SynthDefs in this project.

Hardware and tools

  • ESP32 boards and ESP-NOW by Espressif. Firmware built with the Arduino-ESP32 core (LGPL-2.1) and ESP-IDF (Apache-2.0).
  • Installing firmware in the browser: esptool-js (Espressif, Apache-2.0).
  • Local app: Python with pyserial (BSD). QR codes for playing together: qrcode-generator (Kazuhiko Arase, MIT).
  • Online rooms: PeerJS (MIT) and its free public matchmaking server.
  • Gestures: learning by example as in Wekinator (Rebecca Fiebrink).

Gestures

Teach the organ your moves. Pick the pipes, hold a pose while recording, and repeat for a few poses (teach a silence pose too: no pipes picked). Map chooses what the model listens to and what it plays (pipes, tempo, loop); then Train, and hold to play. In a room, everyone can do this on their own phone.

No motion sensors or game controller support here. Open the room link on a phone, or use Chrome on a computer for a PS5 controller.

PS5 controller: plug it in by USB, or pair it over Bluetooth first. Other controllers give sticks and triggers only.

MIDI keyboard & pads

Plug a MIDI keyboard or pad controller in by USB. Held keys open their pipes straight away, on top of the pattern; the sustain pedal keeps them open. ● Rec writes what you play into the grid while it plays.

MIDI needs Chrome or Edge on a computer.

The browser asks once whether this page may use MIDI devices.

The hub runs older firmware: update it to 1.3 in More ▾ → Boards to play the real pipes live. Until then keys play the simulated sound.

Boards: hub & pipes

Set up the organ's boards one at a time over USB. The hub is the board that stays plugged into this computer to play; each pipe board drives one pipe, A–H. Give every pipe its own letter: the hub then finds them by itself.
